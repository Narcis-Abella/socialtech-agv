## v41 relay integrity and scoring error

**Measured:** v41's first probe events were scan 1, pose 1, then scan 2, cloud 1, pose 2. The `ParticleCloud` header uses AMCL node time, not scan time, so the probe's cloud numbering here follows publication order. There are 28 clouds for 29 poses; the first pose had no cloud. The old observer paired its first cloud with pose 1 by nearest receive time, then shifted subsequent clouds against poses and marked the last scan pending. Thus the 28 observer scores in v41 cannot be used for localization accuracy or gate assessment, even though they contain no acceptance event. The causal pair guard added to `amcl_acceptance.py`/`amcl_ros_observer.py` refuses a cloud paired with an earlier-received pose. It has a regression test; it has not been replay-validated on the NX. Cross-topic DDS receive reordering can conservatively reduce scorable updates.

**Read in pinned Nav2 1.3.13 source:** [`globalLocalizationCallback`, `nomotionUpdateCallback` and `laserReceived`](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/1.3.13/nav2_amcl/src/amcl_node.cpp#L2954-L3013) show that global reset sets `pf_init_=false` and a no-motion request sets `force_update_=true`. In [`laserReceived`](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/1.3.13/nav2_amcl/src/amcl_node.cpp#L3258-L3339), the first post-reset callback takes the `!pf_init_` branch, which does not clear `force_update_`; the cloud is conditional on `!force_update_`, while the pose can still publish. **Inference supported by v41's first-event sequence:** requesting no-motion before its first scan suppressed that first cloud. Later callbacks clear the flag and the relay's pose acknowledgements serialise the 29 scans. A first-scan exception or prewarm is untested; no complete 1× score is claimed.

## Interpretation and open limits

The corrected, complete 0.25× runs show weak stationary global-search behavior under the tried population, geometry and fixed observable gate. The wrong modes are not a mere reference timestamp artifact: point-time controls retained them. Nav2's uniform free-cell/yaw initialization, zero odometry motion, and zero random-recovery coefficients in most controls plausibly limit coverage; this mechanism is an inference, not a proof of general AMCL inability. The 1× failures are integration/throughput/observability limits, including first-cloud suppression and earlier dropped updates, and must not be counted as localization failures. Neither a calibrated sensor band, independent held-out starts, wrong-map/outside-map controls, nor a valid 1× latency/resource distribution exists. No end-to-end AMCL handoff from a global 3D method has been run.
